Before You Send: 3 Scripts for Difficult Messages

Know what you mean. Find the words. Choose a starting point for saying no, asking for clarity, or apologizing, and adapt it to your situation.

Start with the one you need today.

01 / Say no

A clear answer without a paragraph.

When someone asks you to cover another shift or change your plans, and your answer is no.

What usually gets typed
I am so sorry, I wish I could cover Saturday but my cousin is visiting and I already promised my kids we would do something and I feel terrible about it. Maybe if it was any other weekend I totally would. Please do not be mad.
What the receipt says

A real Social Receipt read of that draft.

VerdictThis needed space. You almost overplayed it.

Read strength78

Best moveCreate space

Send this instead
Direct
I can't cover this one.
Warmer
Thanks for asking. I'm not available for this one.
Firmer
I can't cover Saturday. My answer is no for this one.
Why this works

Offer an alternative only if you genuinely want to follow through. If your answer is yes, say yes; a tool should not turn it into a refusal.

02 / Get clarity

Ask before filling in the blanks.

When a vague message such as “Can we talk tomorrow?” has you imagining what it means.

What usually gets typed
Hey, about tomorrow. I keep thinking about what you might want to talk about and I am kind of spiraling. Did I do something wrong? Are we okay? Please just tell me it is nothing bad.
What the receipt says

A real Social Receipt read of that draft.

VerdictYour read is anxious, not accurate. Wait 12 hours.

Read strength22

Best moveWait

Send this instead
Direct
Sure. What would you like to discuss so I can come prepared?
Warmer
Of course. Is there anything you'd like me to think about before we talk?
Firmer
I need a topic before tomorrow so I'm not guessing. What's it about?
Why this works

A short message cannot tell you someone's intentions. Ask for the information you need, or wait for the conversation if you don't need an answer right now.

03 / Apologize

Name what you did.

When you want to repair something you said or did.

What usually gets typed
Sorry. Sorry, sorry. I know I keep saying it but I feel awful and I need you to tell me we are good because I cannot stop thinking about it. Please forgive me.
What the receipt says

A real Social Receipt read of that draft.

VerdictOne apology lands. The rest is asking for reassurance.

Read strength89

Best moveWait

Send this instead
Direct
I'm sorry I snapped at you. That wasn't fair.
Warmer
I'm sorry I snapped at you. You didn't deserve that, and I'm sorry I made things harder.
With repair
I'm sorry I snapped at you. That wasn't fair. Next time I'll step away before I answer, and I'd like to hear how it landed.
Why this works

Add a repair you can actually keep. You can take responsibility for your behavior and still discuss your own concerns afterward.

Your 20-second check.

  1. What do I want this message to do: decline, clarify, or repair?
  2. Does the wording say that directly?
  3. Am I guessing at their intentions?
